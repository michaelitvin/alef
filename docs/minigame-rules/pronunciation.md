# Pronunciation

## Spelling for the voice

- Use the newest model that speaks Hebrew well, with the language code "he".
  Example (duel): eleven_v4 with language_code "he". The parent: "v4 generally sounds better than v3".
- Check the nikkud of each word in a dictionary before you make audio. Use the same correct spelling everywhere in the
  app. Example (duel): צֵירֶה, פַּתָּח.
- Before you generate a word, look for an approved recording of it in the other games, and use that file. Also use their
  pronunciation fixes.
  Example (book 2): אוֹטוֹבּוּס and אַנָּנָס had the wrong stress again; the duel had fixed both. Book 2 now plays the duel's files.
- You can change the spoken text to get the correct sound. Do not change the text on the screen.
- Test the spoken text with and without nikkud, and keep the version that sounds correct. Full nikkud can make the
  voice say a silent shva. Without nikkud, a word that has more than one reading is often read wrong: add nikkud (or a
  stress mark) to those words only.
  Example (clock): "שְׁתַּיִם" was spoken "shetaim" instead of "shtaim".
  Examples (clock): "הארוך" was said "haarach", "סופר" "sopar", "מעולה" "ma'ala", "השען" "hash'en".
  Example (duel): אַבַּטִּיחַ (wrong dagesh) gave "abatiach". The correct spelling is אֲבַטִּיחַ.
- Make the voice say the natural spoken form, even when the formal form is different. Say letter names in the modern
  spoken form: פֵּי, פֵי, טֶת, חֶת, וֶט, חַף; spell them so that the voice says that form.
  Example (clock): "לשתיים" is said "leshtaim", not "lishtaim".
  Examples (duel): צֵירֶה is spoken from "צְרֶה." (else it sounded "tseire"). פַּתָּח is spoken from "פַּתָּךְ." (the final ח was too
  guttural). שִׂיא is spoken from "שִׂי" (else it sounded "sia").
  Example (book 2): the parent rejected "teit", "veit", "pe", "kaf" (for chaf), and צָדִי with the stress on the last syllable.
- The voice does not see a missing dagesh: it reads ב as "b" and פ as "p". Spell a "v" sound with ו (וָה), and test each
  "f" spelling by transcription.
  Example (book 2): בָ, the "va" syllable, was spoken "ba".
- A lone letter with a vowel can be read as the letter's name. Add the vowel letter (גִּי, not גִ).
  Example (book 2): גִ was spoken "gimel".
- Write each syllable for the voice in Hebrew letters, not in Latin letters or IPA. The voice can read Latin letters as
  English letter names, and a Latin "ro." gives an English r and vowel. Use a sign such as the rafe where it helps:
  פִֿי. Do not use IPA alone for a single vowel. Do not put an accent tag on a single syllable.
  Example (book 2): "fi." became "F, I" and "ru." became "R, U"; "/fi/" read the slashes. "[Israeli accent] רָ" was
  spoken two times.
  Example (duel): "/a/." gave 5–6 bursts of extra speech.
- Keep a take of a Hebrew r syllable only if a listener hears a guttural (uvular) r each time you ask. Some Hebrew takes
  give a rolled r.

## Stress

- To move the stress in a Hebrew word, try a meteg first. Example (duel): "אֽוֹטוֹבּוּס".
- To move the stress in a loanword or a slang word, write IPA in slashes inside the Hebrew text.
  Examples (duel): "/ˈmeɡa/ כִּשּׁוּף", "/anaˈnas/", "/baˈʃvunɡ/".
  Caution: IPA can give an English accent. Example (duel): the bus in IPA sounded English.
- To move the stress to the first syllable of a two-syllable word, write the vowel long: צָאדִי.
- This voice often puts the wrong stress on feminine future verbs (תִּתְפְּסִי, תִּגְרְרִי, תִּזְרְקִי were said "titPEsi",
  "tigREri", "tizREki") and says the ־ְתְּ past forms as the masculine ("nitsachta"). Prefer a neutral wording: the first
  person plural ("נִצַּחְנוּ אֶת הַמִּפְלֶצֶת"), "בּוֹאִי נִתְפֹּס", or a feminine form that the voice says right ("אַתְּ אַלּוּפָה").
  A plain spelling or a meteg on the last syllable (תִּגְרְרִֽי) can fix a future verb. Keep the final-ט trick ("ניצחט") only
  when the parent approves the take: he heard it as "Nitshat". Example (book 2).

## Punctuation

- A comma or "!" makes the voice stop, and "!" also makes it shout. Do not put "!" in the text. Do not put a comma inside
  words that are said in one breath. If a word is read wrong without the stop, change the words: do not use the stop to
  fix the reading.
  Example (clock): "שָׁלוֹם, שָׁעָן!" was said "shalom. sha'an."; without the comma, "שען" was read "sha'on" (clock). The line is
  now "אוֹי, הַשָּׁעוֹן שֶׁלִּי מְבוּלְבָּל לְגַמְרֵי".

## When a word stays wrong

- When a word keeps the wrong stress or vowel after several spellings or a retake, change the words of the line, not
  the takes. Do not retake again and again.
  Known in book 2, wrong in any spelling: עוֹד (an odd stress), קְצָת (read "katsat"), בָּאוֹסֶף (sounded off), קְטַנָּה.
  Example (clock): "סדנה" kept the wrong stress, so the welcome line now says "חֲנוּת הַשְּׁעוֹנִים".
  Example (clock): "הַשָּׁעָה עוֹד שָׁלוֹשׁ" stressed עוד in every take, also with a contrast added; without עוד the stress
  falls on שלוש.
- Expect hard words. Give them more takes and more spellings in each round. Put all rounds in review.
  Example (duel): צֵירֶה needed 5 rounds and more than 25 takes.
- If only one take says a word wrong, make a new take. Do not respell the word for all takes before the parent hears the
  respelled takes.
  Example (clock): one ארבע take had an Arabic-sounding guttural ayin. A respelling "ארבא" was made for all takes; the parent
  approved the old plain takes, and the respelling was reverted.

## Check a take

- Check a take with an open question or a measurement, never with a question that names the expected answer: a
  question that names the answer gets that answer back.
  - Ask a listener "what is said, how many syllables, any letter names?", not a question that tells it to expect one syllable.
  - Check each feminine take with an open transliteration that marks the stressed syllable in capitals (no expected
    text given), not only a transcript without nikkud: the transcript shows neither gender nor stress.
  - Check the stress of a two-syllable take by measurement (the loudness, length and pitch of each vowel), not by asking
    a listener "TSA-di or tsa-DI?". Calibrate with a take that the parent rejected.
  Example (book 2): the listener said "first syllable" for every tsadi take, also for the rejected ones; the measurement
  agreed with the parent's ear.
